00Does this version change your Stripe setup?

You said Stripe is already connected and tested. Short answer: nothing to redo in Stripe, but two things in the code were broken and are now fixed, and both need a redeploy.

Nothing changes in the Stripe dashboard. Same webhook address, same signing secret, same five events, same key. You do not need to touch the endpoint you already made.

Two faults were found while writing this guide. Both were in the path that money travels down, and neither would have shown up in a first test:

  • Month two never arrived. Stripe changed the shape of its invoice data in March 2025 (the version they call "Basil"). The subscription reference moved. Our webhook read the old place, found nothing, answered Stripe with a cheerful "200 OK" and did nothing at all. A customer would have paid in month one, worked happily, then been charged again in month two and had no credits added. The same fault silenced failed-payment alerts. The code now reads both the old and the new shape, so it works whichever version your account is on.
  • Pay-then-sign-up left customers on the free plan. Your pricing page asks for payment before the customer creates a login — that is the normal order and it is the right one. The payment is parked against their email address, and a database function called claim_pending_subscription is supposed to hand it over when they sign up. It has existed since September and nothing ever called it. So a customer could pay £79 and still be on the free Sandbox. Sign-up now calls it, and migration 008 goes back and rescues anyone already stranded.
So, two things to do. Run supabase/MIGRATION_008_one_off_sandbox.sql in the Supabase SQL editor, and redeploy the stripe-webhook function with the new file from this package. Everything in this guide assumes you have. If you only do one thing today, do these.

01The journey, in the order a customer meets it

Read this once before touching anything. Every setting later in the guide exists to make one of these steps work.

What they seeWhat happens behind it
1 · Pricing pageFour editions. Sandbox is free, Growth and Scale have a "Choose" button, Enterprise says "Talk to sales" because it is not sold by card.
2 · Checkout form
checkout.html		Your own page, on your own domain. Collects name, company, country, and the three tick-boxes that record their acceptance of your terms. No card details here — that matters, because card details on your own page would put you in a far heavier compliance bracket.
3 · Stripe's payment pageThey are sent to Stripe. Stripe collects the card, handles 3-D Secure with their bank, and takes the fraud risk. Your create-checkout-session function builds this page, reading the price from your database so it can never disagree with your pricing page.
4 · Money movesStripe charges the card and tells your stripe-webhook function. That function writes the plan and the credits into your database. This is the only step that gives them anything — a payment Stripe accepted but the webhook missed is a customer who paid and got nothing.
5 · Success page
success.html		"You're on Growth." It tells them to create their login.
6 · They create a loginThey sign up with the same email they paid with. The parked payment is claimed and their workspace is on the plan they bought, with its credits. (This is the step that was broken.)
7 · Every month afterStripe charges the card again and sends invoice.payment_succeeded. The webhook rolls their allowance over: new credits, unused ones banked up to the cap. (This is the other step that was broken.)
8 · A card failsStripe retries over about two weeks and emails them. Your database marks the account past_due but does not cut them off — an expired card is not a decision to leave.
9 · They cancelStripe sends customer.subscription.deleted. The account drops to a read-only Sandbox: they can still open and export everything, but cannot run new analyses. Credits they bought in packs survive.
One thing worth understanding. Stripe never tells your website that a payment worked — the browser can be closed, the wifi can drop, a chancer can forge the "success" page. Stripe tells your server, privately, in a signed message. That message is the webhook. It is why the webhook matters more than anything else here, and why both bugs above were webhook-shaped.

02Part A · The settings to make in Stripe

About forty minutes, once. Do them in a sandbox or test mode first — the toggle is at the top of the Stripe dashboard. Each one says what it is for, so you can skip anything you have already done.

A1 · Your business details

  • Settings → Business → Public details. Business name CodeIQ Holdings Ltd, support email info@codeiqholdings.co.uk, your website, and your address. This is what appears on the payment page, on receipts, and on the customer's bank statement. A payment from a name people do not recognise is the single most common cause of a chargeback — someone sees "CODEIQ" on their statement, does not remember it, and rings their bank.
  • Set the statement descriptor to something obvious, such as CONTRACTIQ. Up to 22 characters, letters and spaces. It is what the cardholder actually sees. Use the product name, not the company name, because the product is what they bought.

A2 · Branding

  • Settings → Business → Branding. Upload the ContractIQ logo and icon, set the accent colour to your teal and the button colour to your blue. The payment page, the billing portal and every emailed receipt use these. Ten minutes here is the difference between "a professional company" and "a form I am not sure I trust with my card".

A3 · Tax — do this even though you are not VAT registered

  • Settings → Tax. Confirm your head office address (103 Battalion Drive, Wootton, Northampton NN4 6RX) and choose a preset product tax code — Software as a service (SaaS). Checkout will not work until you do. The code asks Stripe to calculate tax on every sale, and Stripe refuses that request if it does not know where you trade from. If you skip this, customers see "Checkout is not configured for tax yet" instead of a payment page. That message is deliberate — it tells you exactly what to fix.
  • Add no registrations. Leave the Locations list empty. Stripe only charges tax where you have told it you are registered. With no registrations it calculates zero, which is correct: you are not VAT registered, and your Legal Centre now says the price shown is the price payable. It also means Stripe Tax costs you nothing — their fee applies to transactions in jurisdictions where you are registered.
When you do register for VAT (currently at £90,000 of taxable turnover, but check the threshold at the time), come back to this page, add the UK registration, and tell me — the prices, the checkout page and two clauses in both Legal Centres have to change together, and there is 30 days' notice to give.

A4 · The emails Stripe sends for you

  • Settings → Business → Customer emails. Turn on receipts for successful payments and emails for refunds. You have no email system of your own for billing. Stripe's receipts are well-designed, carry your logo, and cost nothing. Without them a customer has no record of what they bought.
  • Settings → Billing → Subscriptions and emails. Turn on the emails for failed payments and for cards about to expire. An expired card is the most common reason a paying customer silently stops paying. Stripe will chase it for you.

A5 · What happens when a card keeps failing

  • Settings → Billing → Subscriptions and emails → retries. Leave Smart Retries on, and set the end of the retry schedule to cancel the subscription. Stripe retries for about two weeks, picking times a payment is more likely to succeed. If it never does, cancelling sends customer.subscription.deleted, which puts the account into read-only — the behaviour you chose. The alternative, "leave unpaid", would leave someone marked past_due with full access for ever.

A6 · The billing portal — how customers cancel without emailing you

  • Settings → Billing → Customer portal. Turn on: cancel subscription, update payment method, update billing details, and invoice history. Add your Legal Centre link as the terms of service link, and your site as the return link. Your pricing page says "cancel at any time". Without this, cancelling means emailing you and waiting — which is both a worse experience and a reason for a chargeback. With it, cancelling takes them thirty seconds and tells your database automatically.
  • Leave "switch plan" OFF. Your plans are built in code at the moment the customer pays, not as saved Stripe products, so the catalogue has no Stripe price IDs to match. If a customer upgraded Growth → Scale in the portal, Stripe would charge them the higher amount and your app would leave them on Growth. Until there are real Stripe prices and those IDs are in plan_catalogue, upgrades go through you.
  • On the same page, turn on the portal login link and copy it. It is a billing.stripe.com address. A customer enters the email they paid with and Stripe emails them a link in — no password, nothing for you to build. Put it in your welcome email and on your contact page as "manage your billing".

A7 · The webhook (already done — two things to check)

  • Confirm the five events are still listed: checkout.session.completed, invoice.payment_succeeded, invoice.payment_failed, customer.subscription.deleted, customer.subscription.updated. Your screenshot earlier said "listening to 6 events". A sixth does no harm — the function ignores anything it does not recognise — but it is worth knowing which, in case one of the five was swapped out.
  • Note the API version shown on the endpoint. Nothing to change: the fixed webhook now handles both the old and the new shape. Worth knowing because it is what caused fault one, and because it will matter again the next time Stripe changes a payload.

A8 · The secrets in Supabase

Supabase → Edge Functions → Secrets. These are project-wide, not per function.

SecretValue

03Part B · The dress rehearsal

Do the whole journey yourself with a fake card, in test mode, before any real customer sees it. It takes about half an hour and it is the only way to know the chain works end to end. Keep the Supabase SQL editor open in another tab.

Check you are in test mode before every step: the Stripe dashboard shows a banner, and your key starts sk_test_. Real money cannot move while both are true.
  1. Sign up for a free Sandbox with an address you have never used. Use the Start free button, which now opens the create-account form directly. Use the plus trick — you+test1@codeiqholdings.co.uk — so you can do this as many times as you like with one inbox. Then check it: 
    select name, plan, credits_included, billing_email
  from accounts order by created_at desc limit 1;
    Expect sandbox and 100.
  2. Open the app and run one analysis. You are checking the free experience works before asking anyone to pay for it. Ten credits should disappear.
  3. Go to the pricing page, choose Growth, and fill in the checkout form with a different new email, you+test2@codeiqholdings.co.uk. A different email on purpose: this tests the normal journey, where somebody pays before they have an account. It is the path that was broken.
  4. Pay with 4242 4242 4242 4242, any future expiry, any 3-digit CVC, any postcode. Stripe's test card. It always succeeds.
  5. You land on the success page. Now check the money landed in the database. 
    select id, type, received_at from stripe_events order by received_at desc limit 5;
select email, plan, claimed_at from pending_subscriptions order by created_at desc limit 5;
    You want a checkout.session.completed row in the first, and a row in the second with claimed_at still empty. That is the payment parked and waiting.
  6. Create a login with that same email, then run the query again. 
    select a.plan, a.credits_included, a.stripe_subscription_id, p.claimed_at
  from accounts a
  left join pending_subscriptions p on p.email = lower(a.billing_email)
 order by a.created_at desc limit 1;
    Expect growth, 500, a sub_… id, and a claimed_at with a time in it. Before this version, that query returned sandbox and 100 — the customer had paid and got nothing. If you see that, migration 008 has not been run.
  7. Inside the app, buy a credit pack — Settings → Buy 100 credits — with the same test card. A different route through the code: the buyer is signed in, so the credits go straight to their workspace. Expect the balance to go up by 100 and a line in the credit history.
  8. Test the renewal without waiting a month. In the Stripe dashboard, Billing → Subscriptions → test clocks: create a clock, attach a new test subscription to it, then advance it by a month. This is the only honest way to prove month two works, and month two is where the first fault lived. After advancing, the account's credits should be back to 500 and a rollover line should appear in its credit history. If you would rather not learn test clocks, at minimum satisfy yourself that the renewal arrives in the webhook log as a 200 with a "renewed" line — the function logs one.
  9. Test a failed payment. Start a second test subscription using 4000 0000 0000 0341. This card attaches successfully and then fails when charged — exactly what an expired card does in real life. Expect billing_status = 'past_due' on the account and, importantly, the customer can still work.
  10. Cancel the subscription in Stripe, then look at the account. 
    select plan, credits_included, billing_status from accounts
 where billing_email = 'you+test2@codeiqholdings.co.uk';
    Expect sandbox, 0 credits. Sign in as that user: the red banner appears, contracts still open and export, and Run analysis is refused. That is read-only working.
  11. Last, cancel through the billing portal link rather than the dashboard, as a customer would. Proves A6 is wired up and that the same thing happens when they do it themselves.

If all eleven behaved, you have proved

  • Someone can buy without an account, and get what they paid for when they make one.
  • They are charged again next month and their credits come back.
  • A failed card chases itself and does not lock anyone out on day one.
  • Cancelling leaves them their data and takes away the AI, which is what your legals say.

04Part C · Going live

Only when Part B passed. Twenty minutes, in this order — the order matters.

  1. Activate the Stripe account properly if you have not: company number, your identity documents, and the business bank account the money is paid into. Stripe will not pay out until this is complete. Allow a day or two for checks.
  2. Switch the dashboard to live mode and create the webhook endpoint again. Same address, same five events. Test and live are two separate worlds. The endpoint you already made exists only in test mode and will never see a real payment.
  3. Copy the live signing secret and the live secret key into Supabase, then set STRIPE_ALLOW_LIVE to true, then redeploy all four functions. The order matters: the key alone, without the flag, refuses to take money. That is the seatbelt working, not a fault.
  4. Buy Growth yourself, with your own real card, on the real site. The only test that proves the live chain. Expect £79 to leave your account.
  5. Refund yourself in the Stripe dashboard, then cancel it. A full refund costs you nothing in fees on a UK card. Cancelling is also your test of the live cancellation path.
  6. Check the payout. Stripe pays out on a rolling schedule — the first one takes about a week for a new account, then it settles into a few days.

06Part E · When something goes wrong

Symptoms you might actually see, and where to look. Start every one of these in Stripe → Developers → Webhooks → your endpoint, where every delivery is listed with the response it got.

SymptomAlmost alwaysWhat to do
"Checkout is not configured for tax"Stripe Tax has no head office addressStep A3. Thirty seconds.
Webhook shows 500 "Webhook secret not configured"STRIPE_WEBHOOK_SECRET missing, or the function was not redeployed after it was addedAdd it, redeploy, then use "Resend" on the failed delivery.
Webhook shows 400 "Signature verification failed"The test secret is in Supabase but the event came from live mode, or the other way roundMatch the mode of the key, the mode of the endpoint, and the secret.
Webhook shows 404The function is deployed under a different name than the URL you gave StripeThe function must be called exactly stripe-webhook. A name cannot be changed after deployment — delete it and deploy again.
Customer paid, still on SandboxThey signed up with a different email than they paid with
select * from pending_subscriptions where claimed_at is null;
Find their row, then ask me to hand it over to the right workspace — it is one safe SQL statement, but it should be written for the specific account rather than guessed.
Month two: charged, no creditsThe webhook was not redeployed with this version's fixRedeploy stripe-webhook, then "Resend" that invoice.payment_succeeded delivery from the Stripe dashboard. The credits land immediately.
Webhook shows 500 "Handler failed"A database function is missing — usually a migration that was not runRun the migrations in order. Stripe retries for three days by itself, so there is no rush and nothing is lost.
Everything is 200 but nothing changesStripe is pointed at a different Supabase project than the one your site usesCompare the project reference in the webhook URL with the one in app/index.html.
Stripe retries failed deliveries for three days, and keeps every delivery so you can resend one by hand. A payment is almost never lost — it is usually sitting in that list waiting for you to fix the thing it hit and press Resend.

07Part F · What is deliberately not built

So you know where the edges are before a customer finds one.

  • Upgrades and downgrades inside the app. A customer on Growth who wants Scale has to talk to you. Doing it properly means creating real products and prices in Stripe and putting those IDs in plan_catalogue — half a day, worth doing once you have customers who want it.
  • Annual billing. Monthly only. Annual is usually offered at ten months' price for twelve and is the single easiest way to improve cash flow, but it needs the same price-ID work.
  • Enterprise by card. Deliberate: £700 flat is marked "talk to sales" and invoiced by hand.
  • Your own billing emails. Stripe sends receipts and dunning. You send nothing. That is fine to launch with.
  • Deleting the data of Sandboxes that never convert. Nothing deletes them today. Your Beta terms allow it; decide a period — ninety days after the Sandbox ends is normal — and it becomes a small scheduled job.
  • VAT. Everything is built for not-registered. When you register, several things change together.
